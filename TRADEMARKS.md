## Why official logos are omitted

This is not legal advice. In short: U.S. nominative fair use is stronger for **word marks** used to identify someone else’s product than for shipping their **logo files**. Logos are often also copyrighted artwork. Company brand terms are stricter than the fair-use defense.

Published terms we used:

- [Microsoft trademarks](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/legal/intellectualproperty/trademarks) — logos, app icons, and designs generally require an express license. Third-party app UI should be unique to the developer except for a truthful text compatibility claim.
- [GitHub logo](https://brand.github.com/foundations/logo) — registered trademarks and the Octocat copyright need written permission outside listed uses. Do not imply GitHub created or endorses you. From 2025, Copilot no longer uses a standalone hero icon.
- [OpenAI brand](https://openai.com/brand/) — use names and logos only as those guidelines allow. There is no public grant to put the Codex / OpenAI mark in a third-party desktop launcher.
- [Cursor brand](https://cursor.com/brand) — assets are published for representing Cursor consistently; the page does not grant a license to embed them in another product.
- [xAI brand guidelines](https://x.ai/legal/brand-guidelines) — marks may be used to refer accurately to xAI; logos, if used at all, must be unmodified; no implied endorsement; permission can be revoked.

If a company later grants written permission, only that company’s unmodified official asset should replace the matching original tile.
